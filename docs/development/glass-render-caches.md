# Glass surface cache costs

The Forge Glass/Porthole material mapper caches atlas UV transforms independently of lighting, transparency order and shader vertex formats. Ordinary and diagonal Glass, Portholes and their shared panel paths use this mapper. A hit compares mesh and sprite identity plus scalar rectangle, mirror, border and layout choices. Its reusable lookup is protected by the cache monitor, never inserted into the map and releases its references after every call. Cached keys remain unchanged.

The cache keeps at most 1,024 entries and an 8 MiB conservative retained-mesh budget. Each entry accounts for both original and mapped geometry, key/node overhead and the two optional packed batch arrays on the rendering thread. Charging shared originals repeatedly deliberately favors eviction. An individual oversized result is returned without retention. Resource reload and world unload discard the mapped entries, so an old atlas cannot remain pinned through this cache.

The focused CPU checker compares 96 exact transformations, including mirrored rectangles, raw/atlas input and all three layouts. Its interleaved sixteen-layout probe measures 1,703,936 allocated bytes over 4,096 warmed hits before the change and zero afterward. It also covers byte-driven eviction below the entry ceiling, exact rebuilt output, signed-zero lookup equality, oversized refusal, changed atlas identities and explicit retirement. These are mapper costs; surrounding tile/group preparation, actual draws and hardware frame rate require separate measurements.

The exact standard Forge JAR passes the focused Glass appearance client with OptiFine installed: actual resource reload, joined UV seams, regular/diagonal Glass and Portholes, frame visibility, opacity and dedicated dialogs. The test's default 2 GiB heap exhausted during the full atlas reload; the same JAR completes with an 8 GiB test heap. These focused checks do not constitute the deferred release gate or hardware shader-pack acceptance.
