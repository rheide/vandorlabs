Forge 1.12.2 remains the default and behavioral reference. NeoForge 1.21.1 is under conversion; no modern family is marked complete. A native registration, a successful build, or a shared fixture alone does not establish parity.

The [machine-readable inventory](feature-coverage.json) includes all 57 active generated-catalog registrations and the manually registered block/item families. Programmable-only textures, retired IDs and internal model templates are excluded from registered-block counts. Manual constructor designs, cockpit parts, gear, turntables, navigation tables and items are tracked explicitly.

## Current native adapters

The programmable cube has a native block entity, stable housing choices, per-face material selection, shared dialog controls, server-owned menu authorization, bounded settings transport, configuration-preserving drops and creative picks. Filesystem materials now retain stable IDs through missing-file fallback and update already-open thumbnails and immutable model UV snapshots when artwork dimensions change. Dedicated save/restart and live cube render/reload checks passed. Joined Stretch/Fit now uses the shared bounded plane policy and immutable client-thread snapshots. Section workers enqueue positions and read only snapshot data. Native emitted geometry and actual menu edits cover six faces, all six saved facings, Fit margins, material/layout splits and rendered Stretch/Fit versus Tile artwork. Carried assemblies, shader acceptance and custom material definitions remain required.

The programmable viewscreen and six console/input/diagonal variants use the shared saved schema, display policy, compact animation decoder, pause-aware clock, housing/surface meshes and dialog controls. Console surfaces retain independent settings and redstone rows. Full viewscreens also accept filesystem surface and housing choices through the actual selector, retain high saved IDs and update emitted UVs and open thumbnails through missing-file and dimension reloads. Native Redstone overlays and the eight-row editor use shared toggle/slider/pulse policy, partial-channel latch banks and native ray interaction. Native wireless endpoints use shared channel membership/network/signal policies, deferred loaded-world registration, directional analog/digital ports and exact editing sessions. Plain wireless items retain the original pick/drop and fresh-placement behavior. Native editing preserves the held-item Adventure support-block permission rule, alongside exact menu/tile, loaded-world and reach checks. Their remaining integration and rendering requirements are recorded above.

The plain Programmable Matter block retains its authored artwork, padded item, nine-ingot recipe and plain loot. Basic matter/alloy ingots and the Configurizer item exist in the modern target. The full matter-production chain and every Configurizer target are separate unfinished work; these items do not imply those machines or workflows are available.

All eleven legacy control IDs have native registrations, including the hidden compatibility variants. The Push Button, Rocker Switch and Programmable Trigger retain their dedicated behavior. They retain scheduled twenty-tick pulse semantics, persistent bank isolation, mounted placement/support, directional power, saved/configured items and exact editing permissions. Actual dialog/press/retrigger checks and all 128 configured item renders passed, including pixel padding and comparison with the legacy dialog. The trigger reuses cube housing storage/rendering with a separate derived visual-state hook. It combines loaded local and channel input, retains exact-level and four-band settings, and renders one visible world material while configured items preview the OFF material. Inherited face settings remain saved data without changing trigger appearance. Its actual four-list dialog, immediate channel edits and 0/3/7/13 artwork are exercised in the native client. Power levers retain native sizes, canonical configured-item identities and exact mounted placement; throttles and wall sliders retain four-step levels, limits and type selection. Their dialogs draft until Done/Enter and discard on Escape, sharing the same layout and transitions as Forge. Native save/restart, 3584 emitted-model cases, 768 configured item renders, actual GUI3/2 reverse cycling and maximum-channel edits passed. Broader mounted-world interactions, shader acceptance and carried integration remain outstanding.

Programmable Slabs and Stairs use shared tessellation, joining, side/per-face material settings, texture mapping, dialog controls and contained-volume policies. Native placement includes configured slab merging into the full cube while retaining the installed snapshot. Actual emitted models match the reference across 600 stair and 108 slab cases, including exact collision unions, material identities, winding, shading and content UVs. Native dialogs exercise both GUI scales, two/eight material tabs, reverse cycling, inherited sides, dormant face restoration and the stair slider/roll controls; 48 configured item variants retain visible padding. Eight actual chunk-cache unload/reload pairs check canonical/custom geometry, immutable worker snapshots, wet saved data and collision/free-volume complements. Rendered owner geometry must already match a forced fresh rebuild. Dedicated restart and real bucket/body/eye/fog/light tests cover water and lava; these do not establish installed third-party-fluid or shader-pack acceptance.

Actual legacy and native item-renderer exports agree across 28 cube/Trigger previews: default and unversioned choices, square and rectangular artwork, all three layouts and dormant face settings. Materials, content UVs, quad shading, GUI transforms and retained data match; all rendered icons preserve the reference slot padding. These checks do not extend the claim to other item families or shader rendering.

## Acceptance gates

Every family needs the same shared behavior on both targets, native persistence and permissions, a subsequent-process save/reload, native client/server round trips, and inspected geometry/dialog/animation output. Broad moving-assembly families additionally require transactional failure recovery, original-snapshot retention, loaded-chunk bounds and dedicated-server checks. External integrations need native equivalents or explicit unsupported status.

Run the root [build orchestrator](../../tools/build_all.py) for both independent Gradle builds and dual-JDK shared contracts. Preserve the existing Forge `verifyRelease` gate against the exact standard artifact. NeoForge startup, runtime, visual and full-family coverage checks remain distinct from compilation. The target is not release-ready while any required family remains partial or unimplemented.

Industrial Table parity retains the canonical hanging end models and the legacy collision proxy separately: the authored end model reflects horizontal strut placement when hanging, while the collision proxy reflects only vertical coordinates. The native adapter matches both existing contracts.
