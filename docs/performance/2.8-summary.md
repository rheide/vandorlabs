# Performance findings and next priorities in 2.8

This report combines the recent dense-build and saved-vehicle measurements with the Vehicle Mode preparation changes. The rendering figures are software-rendered comparisons, not hardware FPS predictions. Earlier results retain their original fixtures and measurement conditions; see [dense-build rendering](../render-performance.md) for the detailed comparisons.

The first three priorities now have a [measured vehicle follow-up](2.8-followup.md), covering primitive terrain collections, obstacle allocation, transaction-scoped preflight reuse, scan scheduling, cargo weight and main/shadow renderer attribution. Its whole-scene controls also show why a faster run alone is not evidence of an FPS gain.

## Verified improvements

| Workload | Before | After | What the result establishes |
| --- | ---: | ---: | --- |
| 64 animated garages, 2,880 sections | 17.82 ms; about 37 MB allocated per frame | 1.76 ms; about 14 KB | Batched rendering and shared pose geometry remove repeated per-section work. |
| 29×29 unclaimed Surface floor | 1,682 face submissions; 5.43 ms | One atlas submission; 0.92 ms | Compatible fixed surfaces can share a draw with identical pixels. |
| 78 stationary opaque ramp cells | 2.64 ms | 0.58 ms | Retained clipped geometry avoids rebuilding settled endpoints. |
| 64 carried walls, light-only refresh | 182 µs; 134 KB | 46 µs; 7 KB | Lighting can refresh independently of retained shader vertex payloads. |
| Saved flat hull, collision step across six poses | About 3.0 ms; 2.22 MB | About 2.3 ms; 1.35 MB | Shallower collision regions and terrain reuse reduce CPU and allocation while preserving movement. |
| Saved 3,325-block craft, stationary shader frame interval | 442 ms | 257 ms | Catch-up prediction/collision work was a substantial part of the delay between draws. Completed rendering remained near 210 ms. |
| Contained-fluid partition, 26 cases | 2,284,318 cells | 19,100 cells | Compact runs retain identical ownership at the original grid resolution. |
| Vehicle rotation preflight, 1,024 identical block states | 4,096 rotation calls | Four rotation calls | One four-turn check per distinct block state replaces one per block. This is a work-count reduction, not a whole-conversion speedup. |

Garage network publication also fell from 2,880 section updates to 576 owner updates per motion command in the 64-garage fixture. Repeating 6,400 unchanged inputs sent no additional updates. Static glass and mechanism collision queries now reject irrelevant cells or search nearby owners rather than unrelated loaded tile maps.

## Vehicle conversion preparation

Vehicle discovery now combines its cell-count budget with an elapsed-time target of two milliseconds per work slice. Tile refresh and lossless rotation validation run as separate resumable stages. Repeated states reuse their four-turn rotation result within that validation. Collision geometry preparation and journal persistence retain their existing workers; world access remains on the server thread.

Once discovery identifies the full craft, a temporary preparation hold pauses that craft's ticking tiles. Their data is refreshed before validation. This prevents a running furnace from making its own snapshot stale while preparation spans several ticks. The durable transfer acquires its hold before the scan hold is released. Cancellation, timeout, failure and world unload release the temporary hold. Native blocks and inventories retain ownership until the recovery journal permits publication.

Vehicle Status displays an indeterminate activity indicator through preparation and transfer. Its last-scanned monochrome model is a fixed 12×12×12 occupancy grid: 216 bytes before NBT overhead. Preview rendering uses that grid and does not construct block models, tile renderers or a synthetic world. The controller retains the preview after parking. The Programmer caches decoded silhouettes independently of full chip payloads.

Vehicle Chips store the exact bounded vehicle snapshot in a compressed payload, separately from the small silhouette. Reading enforces both compressed capacity and the existing expanded snapshot limit. The live 24-block fixture decreased from 4,737 raw bytes to 1,319 stored bytes with an exact round trip; compression ratios depend on the design. This reduces retained inventory and synchronization bytes while preserving a checked round trip. Two full-capacity chips fit within the Programmer's vanilla tile-NBT budget. Large carried-item changes use the existing fragmented snapshot transport when a single tile packet is insufficient.

The two-millisecond target is not a guarantee that every conversion tick completes in two milliseconds. Connectivity grouping, constructing a synthetic view, final NBT encoding, permission/protection callbacks and atomic block publication can still exceed it. A matching before/after trace for the player's full-size craft is needed to quantify total conversion latency and worst-tick reduction.

## Articulated coupler costs

Vehicles whose couplers retain the default **Articulation: No** skip articulated shape collection and keep the original movement-packet layout. An articulated vehicle partitions retained meshes by rigid section and applies section matrices at draw time; changing a joint angle does not rebuild block vertices. Posed entity-collision boxes are reused until the pose or geometry changes.

In the four-section, three-joint turning fixture, clipping the tractor against cached terrain before proposing trailer motion reduced complete pose checks from 7,453 to 3,233. This is a work-count comparison, not a hardware frame-rate claim. Articulated movement still performs additional three-dimensional terrain and self-collision checks; cost depends on collision-box count, nearby terrain and how many joints are blocked.

An unchanged movement update remains 82 bytes. The measured three-joint update is 108 bytes: 14 bytes for the optional topology/count header and four bytes per joint. Exact packet round trips, bounded length rejection and stale/repeated state interpolation are checked independently of the rendering comparison.

## Next biggest wins

The priorities below are based on the remaining measured costs and the code paths still performed synchronously. Expected benefit outside the measured fixture is a profiling hypothesis.

1. **Remaining carried rendering and shader work.** Completed rendering near 210 ms remains the largest measured component of the saved shader scene, even after the frame interval improved. Split the remaining 3,032 tile renders by family and material pass, including screens, glass, dynamic light queries and translucent overdraw. Prefer additional retained geometry or atlas batching where the profile demonstrates repeated work. Compare native and carried scenes under the same camera and shader, and preserve material IDs, depth/order and reload invalidation. Hardware profiling is required before claiming hardware FPS gains.

2. **The remaining client prediction and terrain allocation.** The saved hull still allocates about 1.35 MB per collision step, and sustained shader rendering retains about 4.3 MB of Java allocation per frame. Attribute those allocations and repeated terrain queries across prediction/catch-up ticks. Reuse scratch collections or batch-local immutable terrain results where ownership is clear. Terrain and collision caches must invalidate on relevant world edits; retaining stale obstacles across ticks would trade speed for incorrect movement.

3. **Residual vehicle conversion spikes.** Instrument discovery, connectivity grouping, tile refresh, view construction, adapter validation, encoding, protection callbacks, journal wait, publication and checkpoint separately on a large saved craft. Record total latency, maximum and p95 server-tick contribution, allocations, and bytes/messages. Continue slicing pure preparation before considering changes to publication. Reusing validated immutable data within one transaction is promising; caching a complete parked vehicle across player edits needs a complete invalidation design. Do not spread inventory/block ownership publication across ticks without corresponding durable partial-save recovery.

4. **More fixed geometry in terrain and retained material batches.** The Surface and settled-ramp results show the value of removing steady per-tile submission. Profile the remaining stationary selectable blocks and screen housings before expanding the terrain path. Keep animated surfaces and special glass in the correct pass. Validate geometry, light, six-way orientation, neighbouring edits, shader formats and resource reloads.

5. **Cold fluid construction and cache pressure.** The compact fluid partition has a much smaller cell count, but that alone does not quantify total frame savings. Measure cold build time, retained bytes and cache churn on many mixed wet mechanisms. Further work should target measured construction/eviction costs while retaining fluid contacts, ownership and face order.

The older [programmable-block review](README.md) remains useful historical evidence. Several of its proposed wins have now landed, including stationary ramp caching and additional atlas batching; its original priority order should not be treated as the current 2.8 ranking.
