# Pilot Seat, Taxi and Hover modes

The Pilot Seat controls a craft assembled from connected blocks and at least three landing gear or wheel supports. Taxi and Hover require at least one Rocket Thruster, Ion Drive, Plasma Vent or Impulse Engine. Hover additionally requires at least three Antigravity Plates, Repulsor Arrays or Vertical Hover Thrusters. Taxi Mode is enabled by default: sitting assembles the craft, and leaving attempts to restore it as blocks. Disable Taxi Mode to convert the craft manually with the Configurizer.

Canopy and Canopy Glass share the space above the seat's lower block with its backrest. Either placement order works: holding a canopy item passes clicks on the seat through to placement, including when sneaking. Empty-hand boarding and configuration tools retain their usual actions.

The Configurizer and sneak-right-click open the same Pilot Seat dialog. Valid toggle-channel edits apply automatically after a short typing pause; trigger, seat height and Taxi Mode apply when changed. Invalid channel drafts are ignored. **Close** applies any remaining valid edit and closes the dialog. There is no Save step. **Mode channels...** opens the Block / Park, Taxi and Hover channel lists, which also apply automatically. A failed or oversized craft inspection leaves these settings available in the same dialog while assembly stays disabled. Sneak-right-click the visible Pilot Seat on a captured craft to open that same dialog without holding a Configurizer. Hovering, an unsupported parking heading or an obstructed parking area keeps conversion disabled while seat settings and mode channels remain editable. Settings update the seat without scanning or rebuilding the craft on each edit.

The trigger can be **Disabled**, **Redstone ON** or **Redstone OFF**. ON reacts when any configured channel becomes powered; OFF reacts when all configured channels become unpowered. Each new activation toggles between placed blocks and an assembled vehicle. A held signal does not repeatedly convert a craft. Saving settings and loading a world establish the current signal as the baseline, so they do not trigger a conversion. Channel 0 disables channel activation even when OFF is selected.

Seat height offers **Base**, **+2 px**, **+4 px** and **+6 px**. Left-click selects the next height; right-click selects the previous height with wraparound. The same direction applies to trigger and other cycling option buttons. Base is the default and uses a shorter pedestal. The cushion, armrests, backrest, collision bounds and seated player all follow the selected height. Height and trigger settings survive assembly, parking and world saves.

A Pilot Seat fits one block directly below Canopy Glass or an upright Canopy, including joined widths up to nine. Place either block first. The open-bottom canopy shares the backrest space without replacing the glass, and both blocks remain part of the vehicle. Removing the canopy preserves the seat and restores its ordinary backrest reservation. Each armrest hides independently when a full solid block is directly beside the seat, including in the captured vehicle view.

Canopy and optionally openable Front Canopy Glass / Large Front Canopy Glass remain operable aboard the craft. Click their actual surface with an empty hand to open, close or reverse the motion. Their selected sliding/hinged motion, front profile, mount, large-window hinge and material settings survive assembly and parking.

While seated, your own Pilot Seat does not intercept selection of captured canopy or cabin surfaces. Walls and other blocks still block clicks normally. The same empty-hand operation works for rear hinges and forward or backward sliding canopies above a parked seat.

Programmable Screens in Redstone mode remain clickable aboard a vehicle, including row buttons and level sliders on supported screen and console faces. Their channels share the loaded worldâ€™s channel network: onboard controls can operate placed receivers, and placed controls can operate captured doors, trapdoors, redstone-enabled Canopies, lights and landing gear in the same dimension. Trigger polarity and numeric light levels remain configured per block. Channel 0 remains disabled. Ordinary machine simulation and inventory access remain paused while driving.

Landing gear can retract or extend through its configured channel while driving. Before parking, the conversion prepares fully extended gear and its complete owned footprint, raising the chassis when needed to retain its support height. If the extended gear or raised hull needs occupied space, parking preserves the vehicle and retries after the space is clear. Gear settings and channel lists survive conversion.

Parking accepts headings within **12 degrees** of a multiple of 90 degrees. Successful parking aligns the restored blocks to that cardinal heading and checks the complete placement area before writing any blocks. At an unsupported angle, leaving Taxi Mode still dismounts the player and shows a warning that the craft was not converted back. The vehicle and its contents remain intact. Reboard to adjust its heading, or use the Configurizer when aligned. Obstructed parking also preserves the craft and retries automatically after the obstruction is removed.

Using **Park as blocks** while standing beside or inside an unoccupied craft keeps your position and does not seat you. A player already driving remains in the restored Pilot Seat.

Craft detection respects empty slab faces: a slab occupying the top half of its cell does not connect through its empty lower half to a hangar floor. Bottom slabs still contact the floor normally. Settled ramps and escalators can assemble while extended: the vehicle carries the actual extended segments, collision surfaces, source materials and recovery data. Escalators freeze at their captured tread position while driving. Detection includes only sections owned by a captured controller and never follows those sections into neighboring terrain. Vacated source positions remain recovery data rather than solid craft blocks. Parking keeps the extended pose and resumes normal redstone and nearby-player behavior. Cancelled transfers restore the original geometry and recovery data exactly.


Double-tap **Space** while seated in Taxi to enter Hover. **W/S** controls forward/reverse movement. **A/D** steers in Taxi and rotates the craft in Hover, including while stationary. In Hover, hold **Space** to ascend or **Crouch** to descend; releasing both holds the current altitude. Double-tap Space again to select Taxi only when the craft is less than two blocks above solid ground. Hover cannot convert directly to blocks: descend, select Taxi, then park. Hold **Sprint+Crouch** to leave a hovering craft without parking it. A manually controlled craft stops and holds its current position, heading and height when the pilot exits. An enabled autopilot continues its route after the pilot leaves.

Lift blocks light up while Hover is enabled, with sparse particles matching each thruster family when more than one block above the ground. Landing gear retracts above two blocks of clearance, preserving nearby trapdoor-cover behavior. Captured Vandor buttons, levers and sliders remain usable through their channels in Taxi and Hover.

The Pilot Seat dialog's **Mode channels...** button assigns separate optional channel lists to **Park**, **Taxi** and **Hover**. Channel 0 leaves a mode unassigned. A rising signal selects that mode directly; held signals do not repeatedly convert the craft. Existing toggle-channel configuration remains available separately. Capability, ground-clearance and placement checks still apply to mode requests.

See [Autopilot and waypoints](vehicle-autopilot.md) for route markers, timed stops and the Vehicle Status screen. Autopilot owns movement while enabled and continues after the pilot exits.
