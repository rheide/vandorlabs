# Pilot Seat, Taxi and Hover modes

A craft needs one Vehicle Controller, at least one Pilot Seat, and at least three landing gear or wheel supports among its connected blocks. Taxi and Hover require at least one Rocket Thruster, Ion Drive, Plasma Vent or Impulse Engine. Hover additionally requires at least three Antigravity Plates, Repulsor Arrays or Vertical Hover Thrusters. Taxi Mode is enabled by default: sitting assembles the craft, and leaving the last occupied seat attempts to restore it as blocks when that controlling pilot's seat has Taxi Mode enabled. Disable Taxi Mode on a Pilot Seat to board it without automatic assembly and use the Vehicle Controller for manual assembly and parking.

Canopy and Canopy Glass share the space above the seat's lower block with its backrest. Either placement order works: holding a canopy item passes clicks on the seat through to placement, including when sneaking. Empty-hand boarding and configuration tools retain their usual actions.

The Configurizer and sneak-right-click open a compact height and Taxi dialog for the selected Pilot Seat. Every seat keeps its own height, including while captured. Height changes apply immediately; there is no Save step. Hovering, an unsupported parking heading or an obstructed parking area does not prevent height editing. The selected seat also owns its automatic Taxi Mode toggle. Trigger channels and explicit Park/Taxi/Hover mode-channel lists belong to the Vehicle Controller.

Place a **Vehicle Controller** in the connected craft to select, assemble and park it. Right-click the controller, or use the Configurizer on it, to inspect the selection and confirm conversion. This panel contains the block/support/seat counts, highlighted selection, assembly status, toggle channels and trigger polarity. Three inline rows each contain an optional mode channel list and a **Park**, **Taxi** or **Hover** button. The current mode button is disabled. Selecting Taxi assembles placed blocks; Hover assembles a craft with the required lift and thruster blocks; Park restores a stopped Taxi craft as blocks. Valid channel edits apply automatically; invalid drafts are ignored and Close flushes remaining valid edits. These controls are absent from Pilot Seat settings. New assembly requires one Vehicle Controller and at least one Pilot Seat; multiple Pilot Seats may be occupied at the same time, and the first seated pilot controls the craft until they get up, then control passes to the next seated pilot. Other occupants keep their assigned seats through assembly and parking. A controller is crafted from four iron ingots, four redstone dust and one Programmable Matter Ingot. Previously captured vehicles without a controller retain a Configurizer parking path so their blocks can be restored before adding one.

The trigger can be **Disabled**, **Redstone ON** or **Redstone OFF**. ON reacts when any configured channel becomes powered; OFF reacts when all configured channels become unpowered. Each new activation toggles between placed blocks and an assembled vehicle. A held signal does not repeatedly convert a craft. Saving settings and loading a world establish the current signal as the baseline, so they do not trigger a conversion. Channel 0 disables channel activation even when OFF is selected.

Seat height offers **Base**, **+2 px**, **+4 px** and **+6 px**. Left-click selects the next height; right-click selects the previous height with wraparound. The same direction applies to trigger and other cycling option buttons. Base is the default and uses a shorter pedestal. The cushion, armrests, backrest, collision bounds and seated player all follow the selected height. Independent seat heights and controller trigger settings survive assembly, parking and world saves. When an older craft first gains an unconfigured Vehicle Controller, its primary Pilot Seat's previous trigger and mode channels migrate once to the controller. Explicit controller settings are preserved on later loads; each seat keeps its own height.

A Pilot Seat fits one block directly below Canopy Glass or an upright Canopy, including joined widths up to nine. Place either block first. The open-bottom canopy shares the backrest space without replacing the glass, and both blocks remain part of the vehicle. Removing the canopy preserves the seat and restores its ordinary backrest reservation. Each armrest hides independently when a full solid block is directly beside the seat, including in the captured vehicle view.

Canopy and optionally openable Front Canopy Glass / Large Front Canopy Glass remain operable aboard the craft. Click their actual surface with an empty hand to open, close or reverse the motion. Their selected sliding/hinged motion, front profile, mount, large-window hinge and material settings survive assembly and parking.

While seated, your own Pilot Seat does not intercept selection of captured canopy or cabin surfaces. Walls and other blocks still block clicks normally. The same empty-hand operation works for rear hinges and forward or backward sliding canopies above a parked seat.

Programmable Screens in Redstone mode remain clickable aboard a vehicle, including row buttons and level sliders on supported screen and console faces. Their channels share the loaded worldâ€™s channel network: onboard controls can operate placed receivers, and placed controls can operate captured doors, trapdoors, redstone-enabled Canopies, lights and landing gear in the same dimension. Trigger polarity and numeric light levels remain configured per block. Channel 0 remains disabled. Ordinary machine simulation and inventory access remain paused while driving.

Landing gear can retract or extend through its configured channel while driving. Before parking, the conversion prepares fully extended gear and its complete owned footprint, raising the chassis when needed to retain its support height. If the extended gear or raised hull needs occupied space, parking preserves the vehicle and retries after the space is clear. Gear settings and channel lists survive conversion.

Parking accepts headings within **12 degrees** of a multiple of 90 degrees. Successful parking aligns the restored blocks to that cardinal heading and checks the complete placement area before writing any blocks. At an unsupported angle, leaving Taxi Mode still dismounts the player and shows a warning that the craft was not converted back. The vehicle and its contents remain intact. Reboard to adjust its heading, or use the Configurizer when aligned. Obstructed parking also preserves the craft and retries automatically after the obstruction is removed.

Using **Park as blocks** while standing beside or inside an unoccupied craft keeps your position and does not seat you. A player already driving remains in the restored Pilot Seat.

Vehicle transfers settle Vandor redstone channels after the complete structure is removed or restored. Removed tiles receive no further channel commands, and transferred channel sources retain their state. This prevents moving mechanisms and linked controls from responding to a partially converted craft.

External blocks with tile entities require an explicit data and rotation adapter. The channel transaction does not intercept another mod's private unload hooks or network callbacks. Unsupported machines are rejected before assembly; ordinary tile-free building blocks must pass the state and rotation checks.

Craft detection respects empty slab faces: a slab occupying the top half of its cell does not connect through its empty lower half to a hangar floor. Bottom slabs still contact the floor normally. Settled ramps and escalators can assemble while extended: the vehicle carries the actual extended segments, collision surfaces, source materials and recovery data. Escalators freeze at their captured tread position while driving. Detection includes only sections owned by a captured controller and never follows those sections into neighboring terrain. Vacated source positions remain recovery data rather than solid craft blocks. Parking keeps the extended pose and resumes normal redstone and nearby-player behavior. Cancelled transfers restore the original geometry and recovery data exactly.


Double-tap **Space** while seated in Taxi to enter Hover. **W/S** controls forward/reverse movement. **A/D** steers in Taxi and rotates the craft in Hover, including while stationary. In Hover, hold **Space** to ascend or **Crouch** to descend; releasing both holds the current altitude. Hold **Sprint** for twice the normal Hover cruising speed. Double-tap Space again to select Taxi only when the craft is within three blocks of solid ground and all telescopic landing gear is fully extended. Hover cannot convert directly to blocks: descend, select Taxi, then park. Hold **Sprint+Crouch** to leave a hovering craft without parking it. A manually controlled craft stops and holds its current position, heading and height when the pilot exits. An enabled autopilot continues its route after the pilot leaves.

Lift blocks light up while Hover is enabled. Propulsion blocks light only when their exhaust points against the craft's actual travel direction, including reverse and vertical movement. Hover Sprint continuously emits sparse engine exhaust, including while stationary. Lift blocks, including Antigravity Plates, Repulsor Arrays and Vertical Hover Thrusters, continuously emit their own sparse effects whenever Hover is enabled, even while stationary or unoccupied. Both groups use the block family's particle preset and separate capped emission budgets. Landing gear follows its configured redstone channels and animates with matching collision geometry in both modes. Retraction is allowed only in Hover; extension stops at obstacles. Gear does not retract automatically with altitude. Captured Vandor buttons, levers and sliders remain usable through their channels in Taxi and Hover.

The Vehicle Controller dialog assigns separate optional channel lists to **Park**, **Taxi** and **Hover** directly beside their mode buttons. Channel 0 leaves a mode unassigned. A rising signal selects that mode directly; held signals do not repeatedly convert the craft. Existing toggle-channel configuration remains available separately. Capability, ground-clearance and placement checks still apply to mode requests.

See [Autopilot and waypoints](vehicle-autopilot.md) for route markers, timed stops and the Vehicle Status screen. Autopilot owns movement while enabled and continues after the pilot exits.

Vehicle conversion prepares collision geometry and writes recovery records on bounded background workers. The server rechecks the craft before committing, then spreads lighting, neighbor updates and affected-chunk checkpoints across ticks. It does not force a world-wide save for each boarding or parking operation. A craft stays stationary until its checkpoint completes; slow storage delays that transfer without making the server wait for disk I/O. Animation retains its last exact collision pose while preparation is pending.

## Skis and landing struts

Ski Landing Gear, Landing Struts, and Angled Landing Struts share the wheeled gear's extension slider, redstone channels, and On/Off/Disabled trigger modes. Choose Small (one block) or Large (2x2) in the Configurizer dialog. The angled strut points toward its placement direction.

Matching skis join end-to-end when their size, facing axis, and extension agree. Vertical ski columns share the lowest runner's extension controls; upper runners remain part of the fixed column. Detection follows connected ski sections without following the terrain beneath them.

Ski-only vehicles can Taxi on ice, packed ice and frosted ice while every runner remains above ice. They need no lift hardware for Taxi, and stop before leaving the ice. Plates, struts and mixed ski/wheel supports retain their Hover-only restriction. Install three lift blocks and a vehicle thruster for Hover; extend the gear, stop within three blocks of solid ground, and select Park on the Vehicle Controller to return directly to blocks. For new construction, use [modular landing gear](modular-landing-gear.md); the integrated skis and struts described above are retained for existing saves.


### Passenger chairs

A captured vehicle can carry multiple players in separate Pilot Seats, seated cockpits, Advanced Pilot Chairs, and Programmable Chairs. Every Programmable Chair style and height retains its own seating position while the craft translates or rotates. The first occupied pilot-capable seat supplies driving controls; boarding a Programmable Chair as a passenger does not take control. Luxury Seat and Military Seat do not add vehicle passenger places.

Occupied Programmable Chairs transfer their riders during assembly, and parking restores those riders to their saved chair positions. Each seat can hold one player. Seat assignments are synchronized to the driver and other clients.
